The First. The Only One

School Opened Its First 11th Grade Batch in 2024. Only Student In The Batch Scores 87.4 percent in CBSE 12th Boards, 2026.

By Raktimjyoti Sarma  ·  Pathsala, Assam  ·  CBSE 12th, 2026

11th and 12th grade were probably the strangest academic years of my life.

After finishing 10th boards with 91.2%, I entered 11th grade choosing the Science stream along with Computer Science. Initially, the plan was simple — prepare for JEE, like everyone else and continue studying CS alongside it. But things inside my school were very different from what most people experience.

I was the first batch from my school to study in 11th (and then 12th) in senior secondary section.

And I was also the only student in the entire class. No other student in my batch, nor there were any other streams

I had no classmates. No group of friends sitting beside me during lectures. No classroom noise. No competition inside the room. Every class felt more like a one-to-one discussion between the teacher and me. Sometimes it genuinely felt less like school and more like private tutoring happening inside a classroom building.

At first, it felt unusual. Then slowly, it became normal.

Because there were no classmates, I naturally started interacting more with my juniors. They spoke to me casually, almost like I was part of their batch instead of being their senior. That unintentionally made school life less isolating.

Academically, though, those two years became extremely self-driven.

Last year, i.e. when I was in 10th grade, the Computer Science teacher who had inspired me the most had left the school upon better finding opportunities. The replacement teacher wasn't someone I could truly learn from, so I contacted my former teacher personally and requested him to teach me. He agreed, and for the next two years, nine-tenth of my actual learning in Computer Science happened under his guidance outside regular school hours.

Those two years slowly changed my direction completely.

In 11th grade, I was still trying hard to focus on JEE preparation, as I was distracted by programming, and school academics were to my neck. But over time, I realised my interest in building things was becoming much stronger than my interest in entrance exam preparation. After my 11th final exams, I started seriously building software projects in Python. What initially began as curiosity slowly became the center of my academic life.

By the middle of 12th grade, I made one of the biggest decisions of my school life — I stopped seriously pursuing JEE preparation and shifted my focus almost entirely toward Computer Science, projects and board exams.

That decision was risky, especially in an environment where almost every science student is expected to follow the engineering entrance exam route. But mentally, I already knew where I wanted to go. I was far more invested in understanding systems, programming and technology than memorising formulas for competitive exams.

Even then, academics never completely disappeared from my priorities. I still appeared for JEE Main in the April attempt and scored 83.7 percentile overall, including 98 percentile in Mathematics. But by then, my definition of success had already started changing.

12th boards eventually ended, and the result came out to be 87.4%.

Not terrible, not remarkable either. I was pretty much satisfyed.

Still, those two years gave me something far more important than marks. Studying alone forced me to become independent in a way most school environments never demand. There was no academic crowd to move with, no classmates to compete against, no fixed system pushing me forward every day. Almost everything depended on my own discipline, curiosity and direction.

In many ways, my 11th and 12th grade life felt less like conventional schooling and more like an isolated transition period between school life and the person I actually wanted to become.
