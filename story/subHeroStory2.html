Proof of Competence · 2⨯TKS Selection

The Knowledge Society Selected Him in 2025. He Applied Again next year, and Got Selected Yet Again.

TKS · Youth Accelerator

During 12th grade, I applied to The Knowledge Society (TKS), commonly known as TKS — a global youth accelarator for teenagers program focused on emerging technologies, innovation and problem-solving.

What makes the story funny is that when I applied for the first time, I genuinely did not know it was a selective program.

I found the application on the very last day before registrations were closing on Reddit, and my assumption was that it was probably some open learning community where they would ask a few questions, collect responses and allow people in. Because of that, I approached the entire application process very casually and answered everything in an extremely raw and unfiltered way.

There was no polished strategy behind it.

The application had written prompts along with a video essay submission. I recorded the video, uploaded everything almost at the last possible moment and finally submitted the application at around 11:57 PM. Registrations closed at 12:00 AM that same night.

I didn't think much about it afterward because I never expected the application to be particularly competitive in the first place.

Then, around two days later, I received an email saying that I had been selected.

That was probably the moment I realised the program was far more selective than I had originally assumed.

Despite getting selected, I eventually couldn't join due to a combination of financial and time constraints. At that stage, balancing school academics, board examinations and the program together realistically wasn't possible.

After my 12th board examinations ended in 2026, I decided to apply again.

The second time was much calmer and more deliberate. By then, I had spent another year building projects, learning independently and becoming significantly more serious about Computer Science and technology overall. I approached the process with far more clarity than I had during the first application.

I got selected again.

More than the selection itself, what mattered to me was what it represented. Most of my learning during 11th and 12th grade had happened independently, outside conventional academic structures and mostly driven by curiosity. Coming from a school environment where I was literally the only student in my batch, the experience also felt validating in a very unexpected way.

It made me feel that the unconventional path I had been following was not disconnected from reality after all.
